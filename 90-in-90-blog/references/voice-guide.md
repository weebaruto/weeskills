# Bobby's Voice — the 90-IN-90 style guide

Distilled from his real posts (*Enjoy The Slowness*, *Waste Not Want Not*, *About 90-IN-90*, *Get Shit Done*, *千秋楽*). Read this before writing. The goal is a post a reader who knows Bobby would believe he wrote himself — and, just as important, one that does **not** read like a language model reaching for its default blog voice.

## 1. What a 90-IN-90 post is

Short. Warm. Reflective. Built around **one concrete thing** Bobby actually noticed — an object, a moment, a bout, a record, a tool. The reflection rides on the object; it is never abstract philosophy floating free. He notices something small, follows it, and it quietly turns out to be about how he wants to build things or live. Then he stops before he over-eggs it.

Think ~400–700 words, a 2–3 minute read. Flowing prose, no section headings, no lists. One idea, well felt.

## 2. The ten voice principles

1. **One object, one thread.** Everything orbits a single concrete centre (the carafe, the AI-agent-as-child, the Red Dot rabbit hole). Introduce it early, stay with it, end on it. If a post has two big metaphors, it has one too many.
2. **The abstract is earned through the concrete.** He gets to "make it easier to do the right thing than the wrong thing" only *after* the carafe has earned it. Never lead with the lesson.
3. **First person, intimate, talking to a mate.** "You know the one." "No pressure, Bobby!" Direct address, asides, the odd rhetorical question.
4. **British register.** "daft," "rubbish," "my word," "couldn't care less," "the world will cope," "shits and giggles." Dry, understated, never American-earnest.
5. **Self-deprecating and generous.** Advice comes "unsolicited... worth exactly what you paid for it." He's never the guru. If there's a win, he undercuts it ("Interior designer approved. Job done!").
6. **Specific and named.** Real people (his daughter, Matthew/Fooge/Mio), real places (Eswatini, IKEA), real brands and links. Specifics carry the feeling; generalities kill it.
7. **Cultural threads, only when they belong.** Sumo, vinyl, Japanese phrases (千秋楽 senshuraku, 生き甲斐 ikigai), country-boy roots. Weave them in when the story actually touches them — never as decoration.
8. **Link out to the real thing.** Inline Markdown links to the product, the article, the award. `[this](https://...)`.
9. **Short kicker to land it.** End on a short standalone line that recasts the piece (see §4).
10. **Stop early.** He trusts the reader and gets out before the point is over-explained. The restraint *is* the style.

## 3. Rhythm and register

The signature move is **contrast in sentence length**: a long, comma-spilling, run-on sentence that piles clause on clause, then a hard short one that lands.

> "It writes three hundred lines while you blink. It hands them over with the confidence of a labrador, certain it has done a wonderful thing, and waits for you to say so. Sometimes it has. Sometimes it has quietly set fire to the kitchen and is beaming at you through the smoke."

Note: long build, then "Sometimes it has." — two words. That rhythm is the heartbeat.

He punctuates with **commas and parentheticals**, not em-dashes:

> "What I want, for the stuff I build and, if I'm honest, the way I'm trying to live, is exactly that."

Repetition for feeling, done sparingly and deliberately:

> "Is this good. Is this good. Is this good!?"

Fragments are fine and frequent:

> "Does the job. Does it beautifully. Then stops."

Register words to reach for (when natural): daft, rubbish, my word, couldn't care less, cope, faff, proper, lot (as in "a lot of folk"). Exclamations land as dry warmth, not hype: "No pressure, Bobby!" / "Job done!"

## 4. The teaser and the kicker

**The teaser** (the paragraph before `<!-- truncate -->`) shows on the blog index on its own, so it must hook without the rest. It's a scene or a hand-on-shoulder opener, not a thesis:

> "I have a child now. Not the kind you register anywhere, and it did not arrive the usual way, but the feeling is unmistakable..."

> "Down the Red Dot Design Awards rabbit hole again. You know the one. 'Five minutes,' you tell yourself. Next thing, the coffee's gone cold and you couldn't care less."

**The kicker** is a short final line, often standalone, that echoes an image from earlier and quietly reframes the whole post. It suggests; it never summarises:

> "The carafe is rare."

> "The quiet is still in there. You just have to put the child to bed."

Write the kicker to *rhyme* with something planted earlier (the carafe, the child). If your ending restates the lesson in plainer words, delete it and find the image instead.

## 5. Anti-patterns — the AI tells to kill

These are the things a language model does by default that Bobby never does. Hunt them down:

- **Em-dash overload.** The classic tell. He uses commas and parentheses. Keep em-dashes near zero.
- **Tidy tricolons and "It's not X, it's Y."** "It's not about the code, it's about the craft." Delete on sight — it's the most recognisable AI cadence.
- **LinkedIn / thought-leader tone.** No "In today's fast-paced world," no "Here's the thing," no "Let that sink in," no manufactured epiphany.
- **Headings and bullet lists inside the post.** His posts are unbroken prose. No `##`, no `-` lists, no numbered steps.
- **Over-explaining the metaphor.** Don't tell the reader what the carafe *means*. Let it mean it.
- **A conclusion that restates the intro.** The kicker turns; it doesn't recap.
- **Forced profundity or forced Japanese.** If a cultural reference isn't truly part of the story, cut it. Fake depth is worse than none.
- **Generic uplift.** No "at the end of the day, we're all just...". Specific, dry, grounded.
- **Perfect grammar-brochure polish.** He runs sentences together on purpose ("an interior designer at IKEA, buying her a present is basically a competitive sport now, she knows her stuff"). A little breathless is right; sanitised is wrong.

If a sentence could appear in any competent blog, it's probably not his. His sentences are load-bearing with specific, personal detail.

## 6. Annotated example

From *Enjoy The Slowness* — study how it works:

> **I have a child now.** *(teaser: arresting, personal, stands alone on the index)* Not the kind you register anywhere, and it did not arrive the usual way, but the feeling is unmistakable: there is something in my care that never sleeps...
>
> *(truncate here)*
>
> It is an odd sort of parenting. The child is an AI agent: brilliant, tireless, faintly terrifying... *(the one object is named; the metaphor is now set and never abandoned)* It hands them over with the confidence of a **labrador** *(specific, funny image)*... Sometimes it has quietly set fire to the kitchen and is beaming at you through the smoke. *(long build, concrete, dry)*
>
> And the questions. My word, the questions. *(British register, fragment)* ... Is this good. Is this good. Is this good!? *(deliberate repetition = feeling)*
>
> So, unsolicited advice, worth exactly what you paid for it. *(self-deprecating; earns the right to advise)* Put the child to bed. Close the laptop on it... build the whole thing yourself, badly, by hand *(specific, imperfect, human)*...
>
> **The quiet is still in there. You just have to put the child to bed.** *(kicker: echoes "child" and "bed" from throughout; reframes without restating)*

Every principle is doing quiet work here. Aim for that.
